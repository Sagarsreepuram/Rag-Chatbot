Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (2 October 1869 – 30 January 1948) was an Indian lawyer, anti-colonial nationalist, and political ethicist who led the successful campaign for India's independence from British rule. Known as "Mahatma" (great-souled) and "Bapu" (father), he pioneered nonviolent resistance (Satyagraha) to fight for civil rights, social reforms, and self-reliance. 
Key Data on Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi:
Birth: October 2, 1869, in Porbandar, Kathiawar Agency, British India.
Parents: Father: Karamchand Uttamchand Gandhi (Dewan of Porbandar), Mother: Putlibai.
Marriage: Married Kasturba Gandhi in 1883 at age 13.
Education: Studied law in London (Inner Temple), 1888–1891.
South Africa (1893–1914): Developed his philosophy of nonviolence while fighting against racial discrimination.
Return to India (1915): Joined the Indian National Congress and led major movements including the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), Salt March (1930), and Quit India Movement (1942).
Assassination: Shot on 30 January 1948 in New Delhi.
Publications: Indian Home Rule, Autobiography (The Story of My Experiments with Truth), Young India. 
Key Principles and Actions:
Satyagraha: Truth-force or nonviolent resistance as a method for social and political change.
Social Reform: Advocated for the abolition of untouchability, women's rights, and Hindu-Muslim unity.
Swadeshi Movement: Promoted self-reliance through the use of Khadi (homespun cloth).
